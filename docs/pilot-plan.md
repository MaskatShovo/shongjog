# Shongjog: a focused enterprise pilot

**Problem hypothesis:** field representatives lose time resolving uncertain submissions when connectivity fails. A more reliable submission flow could reduce duplicate handling and follow-up calls.

**Customer:** a regional FMCG distributor with one warehouse and approximately ten field representatives. Recruit through direct introductions and observe existing tools before proposing replacement.

**Four-week validation proposal**

- Week 1: interview representatives and warehouse staff; record consented baseline confirmation times and manual follow-ups. Do not collect shop/customer personal data beyond what is necessary.
- Week 2: integrate the customer's test catalogue and an approved SMS provider; enforce distinct representative/warehouse memberships and gateway authentication.
- Week 3: run a limited route trial and a comparable baseline route. Record environmental differences and outages rather than attributing every gain to the app.
- Week 4: compare completion rate, confirmation latency, duplicate effects, manual calls, message costs, and support burden; ask for an explicit paid trial commitment.

**Pricing hypothesis:** per-active-representative subscription plus separately reported messaging costs. The illustrative app calculator uses editable assumptions and excludes support, hosting, onboarding, taxes, sales costs, and churn. Validate the price with buyers.

**Potential telecom fit:** enterprise distribution and a reliability layer for existing sales-force software. There is no operator partnership or exclusive technical capability. A telecom is a possible distribution/integration partner, not a dependency for the core app.

**Interview pitch:** “I built a failure-tolerant order workflow. Let me cut the connection, reopen the app, and replay a delayed delivery. We will inspect the actual receipt together.”

**Success threshold:** agree it with the distributor after baseline measurement. Do not invent a percentage improvement or confuse received order value with additional revenue.

**Go/no-go:** proceed only when users experience recurring submission uncertainty, integration cost is manageable, and a budget owner commits to pay. Otherwise narrow the use case or stop the commercial hypothesis.
